# TextSentry Privacy Policy

**Last updated:** November 3, 2026

**App:** TextSentry (`org.textsentry.app`). This policy applies to both the Google Play edition and the separately distributed sideload edition.

**Contact and privacy inquiries:** Please open a confidential inquiry through the repository's [security reporting](https://github.com/TextSentry/text-sentry-android/security/advisories/new), or use [project support](https://github.com/TextSentry/text-sentry-android/issues) without including any personal messages, phone numbers, or API credentials.

## What TextSentry does

Before entering the app you must either sign in through Google/Cognito or save your own TypeSafe API key (BYOK). The alternative does not require an account. Before entering a personally provided key, the app requires a dialog acknowledgment that the user is responsible for the provider agreement, data retention and usage charges; canceling keeps personal-key setup closed. Replacing a key in Settings also requires that acknowledgment. Signed-in users analyze messages directly through **OpenRouter's Jev Decisions API** using a RAM-only monthly capped key; BYOK users analyze directly through **TypeSafe Jev**. TextSentry never sends SMS content to its AWS account/key-management backend. There are no analytics or advertising SDKs.

**Google Play edition:** You decide what text to paste or explicitly share with the app. The Play edition does not read incoming messages automatically, cannot send messages automatically, does not request SMS/call-log permissions, and cannot inspect your SMS inbox. You may review and edit an AI suggestion, then manually open your existing SMS app to compose a reply. The SMS app, not TextSentry, sends it after your approval.

**Experimental sideload MMS:** MMS monitoring is off by default and starts with the same explicit **Start Monitoring** action used for incoming SMS, not a separate MMS opt-in. TextSentry requests missing SMS receive/inbox permissions together; Android's hard-restricted `READ_SMS` permission is required to read MMS. If it is not granted, SMS monitoring can continue independently. Once enabled, TextSentry only queries newly downloaded received MMS records in the local system provider (not historical messages), extracts plain-text message parts and subjects; image-only MMS cannot be classified. Only that extracted message text is sent directly to the chosen AI provider for classification; image files, media attachments, audio and video are never uploaded. The result is stored in the same encrypted local history as an MMS decision; MMS messages are never deleted. MMS group messages are not auto-replied to. The app does not become the default SMS app; successful capture depends on the installed messaging app storing completed MMS records in Android's provider. Background scans can be delayed. Neither source SMS nor MMS text goes to TextSentry AWS.

**Sideload update checks:** The sideload edition checks the public TextSentry GitHub rolling prerelease (GitHub REST API) at most once per day on Home open and periodically using Android WorkManager. It reads public release metadata, including the APK asset URL and version code, and caches only the latest build code, download URL and check/prompt timestamps locally. GitHub may receive standard connection metadata such as IP address and request headers. The check does not send SMS/MMS contents, keys, account tokens, subscription status, device identifiers or encrypted history. Newer builds prompt the user inside the app; selecting **Get update** opens the APK URL in the external browser. No APK is downloaded or installed automatically. The Play edition does not include this update checker. Deleting local app data clears update check and dismissal records.

**Optional review email:** In History, tapping the three-dot menu and **Send for Review** opens a draft (with both a URL-encoded mailto body and a text extra for app compatibility) addressed to `support@textsentry.org` using your chosen email app. It includes the selected message's full text and sender, message type, evaluation time, recorded Jev decision and confidence, and reply status. TextSentry never sends this report automatically. Only if you deliberately send the email will your email provider and TextSentry support receive its contents, which may include sensitive personal information. Support email is not a zero-retention AI endpoint, and this feature does not send SMS to the TextSentry AWS backend or add messaging permissions.

**Local exceptions to automatic STOP:** From History, you may explicitly save a sender number or a suggested/editable keyword to protect wanted promotional messages. When you choose **Suggest phrase with Jev** from History, TextSentry asks for explicit confirmation before making one additional request to the same configured Jev provider (OpenRouter in managed mode or TypeSafe with BYOK). Candidate names are extracted locally, and the full selected message is sent directly to that provider so Jev can choose a phrase using a typed Choice decision; Jev does not generate freeform text. This consumes some provider quota. You can edit or decline its suggestion. If Jev is unavailable or unsure, you may enter a phrase manually. No SMS contents or exception candidates are sent to TextSentry's AWS backend, and no rule is saved until you tap Save. Exceptions are stored separately from message history in an app-private, Android Keystore-backed AES-256-GCM encrypted store (up to 100 rules). Local matching happens after ordinary Jev classification and before an SMS send, including a final recheck if rules change during processing. Matching only suppresses automatic STOP; it does not delete messages or reverse a prior opt-out. Clearing message History does not erase exceptions. **Delete app data** destroys both exception records and their encryption key. The Play build remains manual-only.


**Sideload edition:** After you explicitly enable monitoring and grant Android SMS permissions, eligible incoming SMS content and sender numbers can be evaluated through your configured AI provider. Auto-send is separately optional. WorkManager may hold unprocessed SMS and sender data in app-private on-device storage until processing and cleanup. Once evaluated, message content is saved in an app-private AES-256-GCM-encrypted SQLite history. Only Android-confirmed sent replies are retained without automatic expiration or a record-count limit, until you clear History, delete app data, or uninstall. All other decision records expire after 24 hours and are pruned on access and in best-effort periodic background cleanup (Android may delay maintenance). Existing encrypted records are migrated locally on upgrade. History includes the SMS sender, full processed text, evaluation time, TypeSafe model confidence, reply suggestion, Android send result and an explicit indicator that TextSentry did not delete the original SMS. The existing shorter decision outcomes, hashed deduplication records and timestamps are also kept. The full inbox is never read or copied.

## Information handled

- If you opt into Google sign-in/sign-up, Google and AWS Cognito process basic identity information (such as email address and Cognito user ID) and issue access, ID and refresh tokens. The system browser handles your Google password; TextSentry never sees it. The tokens are encrypted with a dedicated Android Keystore AES-GCM key and saved in app-private storage to keep your session between app launches. TextSentry AWS stores account identity and a monthly capped OpenRouter key (with its provider hash, period and expiration) in DynamoDB. The app obtains the key from the authenticated API only into volatile RAM; it is not stored with Cognito tokens or local message history. No SMS message bodies are sent to AWS. Users can sign out to clear the key from RAM; ending an app process also removes it. Provider key records can persist on AWS until rotated or revoked.
- Your TypeSafe API key, confidence threshold and instructions are stored locally on your device. The API key is encrypted using Android Keystore. The local message-history archive uses a separate Android Keystore-backed AES-256-GCM encryption key. The provider and model are not configurable.
- The text you explicitly submit in the Play edition, or eligible SMS body in the sideload edition when monitoring is on, is transmitted **directly to OpenRouter (which routes the Jev decision to TypeSafe) when signed in**, or directly to **TypeSafe Jev** when using BYOK. AWS is used only for Google account authentication and managed-key retrieval, never for SMS body transmission. The provider and model are fixed in this version. The generic chat interface uses a placeholder sender number for manually submitted Play text.
- **Managed OpenRouter requests:** TextSentry's issued OpenRouter keys are assigned a server-enforced guardrail that restricts models to pinned TypeSafe Jev decisions and requires **zero-data-retention (ZDR) endpoints** (`enforce_zdr_other=true`). Key issuance/retrieval fails closed if OpenRouter does not confirm the policy. If no eligible ZDR Jev endpoint exists, inference fails instead of using a retaining route. This is an endpoint routing restriction enforced by OpenRouter, not a claim that OpenRouter has no billing/security metadata. The app still sends message content directly to OpenRouter and TypeSafe for inference, never to TextSentry AWS. **BYOK:** Direct TypeSafe keys are outside TextSentry's managed ZDR enforcement, and retention is governed by the user's separate TypeSafe agreement. Avoid submitting passwords, one-time codes or other highly sensitive text.
- In the Play edition, text submitted with your per-message consent and its probability/suggestion are stored in TextSentry's encrypted local history. The number you enter to compose an SMS is passed only to the installed SMS app selected by Android. TextSentry cannot verify whether the SMS app actually sent the message. TextSentry does not send that number to the AI provider.

## Optional developer diagnostics

A hidden developer mode can be enabled by seven taps on the **AI connection** heading in Settings, followed by confirmation. When enabled, TextSentry retains at most five **complete model request and response JSON pairs** in an AES-256-GCM encrypted local store protected by Android Keystore. These diagnostic request bodies include the original analyzed SMS text and the selected categories and extra instructions; response bodies contain the raw Jev API reply from OpenRouter or TypeSafe, depending on the selected mode. API keys and HTTP authorization headers are not included. This happens only for analyses already requested or permitted by the user, and does not add any network transmission or analytics. Developer mode also permits editing additional natural-language classifier restrictions. Turning it off stops new tracing. Captured diagnostic traces expire after 24 hours (and may be cleared sooner in developer tools, by Clear History, or by Delete app data).

## Your choices and consent

Google sign-in is optional and provides a first-time sign-up or returning-user login without creating a TextSentry password. Signing in automatically tries to obtain a monthly $1-capped OpenRouter key for the account (current free-account beta); it does not activate automatic SMS monitoring or replies or a subscription. Signed-in users use OpenRouter Jev for analysis they explicitly request or separately enable; BYOK users keep using direct TypeSafe Jev. Signing out erases saved tokens from the app, not Google's existing browser cookies or the remote Cognito account; contact project support for account deletion inquiries.

In the Play edition, no message is submitted to AI without checking the consent box and selecting **Analyze**. That choice is reset after an analysis. In the sideload edition, you must separately enable SMS monitoring and automatic sending. You can pause sideload monitoring or delete local TextSentry data in either edition.

## Retention and deletion

TextSentry supports optional Google/Cognito accounts; sign-out and local data erasure do not delete the remote account or Google/AWS sign-in records. The History page keeps Android-confirmed sent responses indefinitely with no count limit, while all other decisions and developer traces expire after 24 hours (on access and best-effort background cleanup). It offers a **Clear history** control; use **Clear local decision history** to remove TextSentry's encrypted history and short outcome log, **Delete all local TextSentry data** to remove all local app records, or Android Settings > Apps > TextSentry > Storage > Clear storage, to erase the locally stored configuration, API key, encrypted account tokens, decision history and deduplication records. TextSentry also cancels scheduled work when local data is deleted. These controls do **not** delete SMS messages from the device's default messaging app; inbox deletion requires default SMS handler capabilities that TextSentry does not currently provide. Deleting local data does not revoke the remote OpenRouter key or delete the remote Cognito/DynamoDB record; it only clears the in-memory key and saved OAuth session on this device. Deleting local data **cannot delete information already transmitted** to your independent AI provider; contact that provider for its deletion procedure.

## Sharing, security and children

TextSentry does not sell your data or share data for advertising. Data transmitted to your chosen provider uses HTTPS. Access to SMS in the sideload edition is controlled by Android permissions. TextSentry is not designed for children, and SMS classification is never guaranteed to be correct.

Sideload requests RECEIVE_SMS for opt-in monitoring and SEND_SMS for separately consented auto-replies, with in-app permission status and requests. SMS deletion would require TextSentry to be your actual default SMS app, which is not offered; a history row's "Not deleted by TextSentry" indicator does not assert whether another app subsequently deleted the message. Android send confirmations are used to distinguish a submitted send from a confirmed sent attempt. The Play edition and sideload edition have different permissions and functionality. This policy may be updated as the product changes; the dated version is published in this repository.
